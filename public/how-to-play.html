How to play

Everything in Waikīkī Lineup, from the first paddle to a shared wave at sunset. Read it once, then go get wet.

The sky is live

The game keeps real Hawaiʻi time. The sun rises and sets when it does at Waikīkī today, so the light you surf in depends on when you show up. The start screen tells you the hour there.

  • Morning and midday: clear turquoise water. You can see the reef and sand under you.
  • Late afternoon: golden light, long shadows, glare on the water when you look toward the sun.
  • Dusk: the skyline lights come on and the last surfers are silhouettes.
  • Night: the moon lights the water. Waves are harder to see coming, and the timing for duck dives and pop-ups is one fifth shorter. In return, every ride scores 10% more.
Tip: if it is your first time, come in daylight. Night surfing is the hard mode.

Before you paddle out

  • Pick a break. Canoes has soft rolling peaks that break both ways. Queens is a little steeper and faster, with longer rides.
  • Pick a board. Open the row with your name to change your board, name and look.
  • Bring a friend. "Copy invite link" gives you a link to the same room. A room holds up to 16 people, and a few regulars are always out.

Paddling and stamina

  • Hold W to paddle. Steer with A and D, or drag the mouse.
  • Tap A and D in turn for sprint strokes. They are faster, and they burn stamina more than twice as fast.
  • The ring at the bottom of the screen is your stamina. It empties as you paddle. When it turns red you are out, and you paddle at half speed.
Tip: paddle out at a steady pace and save the sprint for catching a wave.

Sitting and reading the sets

  • Press S to sit up, and again to lie back down.
  • Sitting refills your stamina five times faster than lying still.
  • You turn about twice as fast sitting, so sit up to spin the board around.
  • Waves come in sets: three to five bigger waves, 10 to 14 seconds apart, then a lull of about a minute with smaller waves in between. Watch the horizon for dark lines.
  • Sit where the other surfers sit, about 110 meters off the beach. That is where the waves stand up.
Tip: the first wave of a set is rarely the best. Let it go, and you get a clear look at what is behind it.

Getting under whitewater

A broken wave will knock you back toward the beach unless you get under it. Press X.

  • Shortboard, duck dive: tap X in the last half second before the whitewater reaches you. In the last fifth of a second it is a perfect dive and you lose no ground. A little early and you are pushed back about 2 meters.
  • Longboard and soft-top, turtle roll: press X just before it hits and keep holding while it passes. You flip under the board and are pushed back 3 to 5 meters.
  • Do nothing and you are washed in 6 to 12 meters, tumbled, and lose a chunk of stamina.
Tip: paddle out during a lull, and go around the breaking part of the wave when you can.

Catching a wave

  1. Pick a wave and lie down (S) if you are sitting.
  2. Turn the nose toward the beach.
  3. When the wave is about four seconds away, hold W. Sprint with A and D if you need to.
  4. When the wave lifts you, press Space to pop up. You have a little over half a second.
  • You need to be near the peak, the steep part that is about to break. Out on the flat shoulder the wave rolls under you.
  • If you miss the pop-up on a steep wave, the nose goes under and you wipe out. On a soft wave it just leaves you behind.
  • A longboard needs a bit less than half the wave's speed. A shortboard needs more than half.
Tip: the easiest first ride is whitewater. Sit a little inside the lineup, paddle toward the beach as the foam arrives, and it picks you up with a longer window to pop up.

Riding

  • Steer with A and D. Ride along the wave, away from the part that is breaking.
  • Shortboard: hold W on the face to pump for speed, S to stall.
  • Longboard and soft-top: hold W to walk toward the nose, S to step back. The board turns less the farther forward you stand.
  • Press E to kick out over the back of the wave when you are done.

Each ride gets a score out of 10. It counts how far you rode, how fast, how many turns you made, and on a longboard, how long you stood on the nose.

Tip: on the nose of a longboard, step back before the wave gets steep. Too far forward on a steep face and the nose digs in.

Wiping out

  • Everyone falls. You are held under for a few seconds, then you come up next to your board.
  • The worst wipeouts are getting pitched on a set wave, and falling where the reef is shallow.
  • A bad wipeout can leave you with a reef cut. That is what the lifeguard is for.

Reef cuts and the lifeguard

  • When you are cut, a red cross shows in the stamina ring and a marker points to the lifeguard tower on the beach.
  • You cannot catch a wave until the cut is treated. Paddle in to the sand by the tower.
  • Lifeguard on duty, 9:00 am to 5:30 pm Hawaiʻi time: the lifeguard seals the cut. It takes about 8 seconds.
  • No lifeguard, before 9:00 am and after 5:30 pm: the tower is empty and you patch the cut yourself on the sand beside it. It takes a little longer.
  • You cannot be cut in your first five minutes in the water, and never more than once in fifteen minutes.
Tip: at night there is no lifeguard and the waves are harder to read. Kick out early instead of riding into the shallows.

Sharing the lineup

The lineup has one rule: the surfer closest to the peak has the wave. While you paddle for a wave, its face glows gold if it is yours and blue if it is someone else's.

How you behave moves your Respect, the dot beside your name. Everyone starts at 60 out of 100.

Tip: if the wave glows blue, let it go. There is another one coming.

Emotes

Hold Q to open the wheel, point at one, and let go. On a gamepad it is D-pad up. On a phone, press and hold the screen.

  • Shaka says hello and thanks.
  • Hoot cheers for someone who is riding.
  • Go tells the others the wave is theirs. If it was yours to give, anyone can ride it with you.
  • Point, Splash and Laugh are just for fun.

Hold Tab to see who is out and how much Respect each surfer has. On a phone, tap with two fingers.

Seven things to remember

  1. Start on the longboard, in daylight.
  2. Sit to rest. The ring refills fast.
  3. Nose at the beach, paddle early, Space when it lifts you.
  4. X gets you under whitewater. Space never will.
  5. Ride away from the breaking part, and kick out with E before the shallows.
  6. Gold wave, go. Blue wave, let it go.
  7. Red cross in the ring: paddle in to the tower.
